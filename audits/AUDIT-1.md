# AUDIT-1 — Phase 1 (login, preflight, `setup --request` and the onboarding bundle)

Adversarial self-audit per CLAUDE.md, "Security audit ritual". Conducted 2026-08-04/05
against the tree from `155fc35` (the AUDIT-0 baseline) through `541c3ec`.

**Assumptions held throughout.** Every operator-supplied value — account id, email,
OU id, OU name, role name, ExternalId, output path, config file — is
attacker-controlled. The operator will be phished. The network is unreliable and
may return a hostile response. Every claim in `DESIGN.md`, the generated `README.md`,
and `docs/` is false until traced to code. **The four generated templates are
executed, not read**: a forged line in a CFN template or a widened statement in an
IAM policy becomes a grant in someone's management account.

**What exists to audit in Phase 1.** `internal/login` (SSO device flow + token
cache), `internal/preflight` (the three-state machine), `internal/awsapi` +
`internal/awsfake` (narrow interfaces and fakes), `internal/config` (TOML config and
ExternalId resolution), `internal/safeio` (confined file I/O), `internal/bundle`
(request validation and the four generated files), `cmd/automat` (five subcommands
and the shared client plumbing). Still absent: any mutating AWS call, the evidence
implementation, the SCP packer, `vend`, `verify`.

**Method.** Findings came from mangling inputs and reading the generated bytes, not
from reading for smells. Every finding was reproduced before it was written down.
Every fix was then **jam-checked**: the fix was deliberately broken and the test
re-run, to confirm the test fails for the reason claimed rather than passing for an
unrelated one. Jam-checks that revealed a weak test are recorded as such below,
including two where the test initially proved less than its name claimed.

**Adversarial agents.** Three independent hostile-auditor passes were run over the
bundle with disjoint lenses — IAM grants, template injection, filesystem and secret
handling — and their findings triaged here in writing. Two of their reports were
partly wrong; the incorrect items are recorded with the reason, because a finding
dismissed without one is indistinguishable from a finding missed.

**Result.** 17 findings: 1 critical, 4 high, 6 medium, 3 low, 3 nits. **15 FIXED,
2 ACCEPTED** with reasons (H1 and N3). Plus four items triaged as NO FINDING or
NOTHING TO AUDIT YET, AUDIT-0's A1 and A2 re-examined and carried forward, and one
agent finding recorded as **WRONG**.

**Fix commits.** C1, H3, M3 → `52e0ac1`, `256094a`. H4 → `01d4b1a`, `77a9be5`.
M4, M5 → `4c2731c`, `894b6f9`. M6, L1 → `ef17c6f`, `5fab62e`. L2 → `1dcda38`.
L3 → `73ab082`. H2, M1 → `541c3ec`. N1, N2 → `b973b2c`. M2 → `2057fc9`.

**For the human: 2 ACCEPTED findings, 2 carried-forward AUDIT-0 acceptances, 3
ratification requests, and one trade — in the two sections at the end.**

## Critical

### C1 — The vendor role could forge the tags the delegation's conditions read. FIXED

The whole safety argument of the bundle rests on tag conditions: the delegation
policy allows policy modification only on resources tagged
`automat:managed-by=automat`, and the role may move accounts only when they carry
`automat:vended-by`. Both are conditions on tags — so the argument holds only if the
delegate cannot *write* those tags onto arbitrary resources.

It could. The role's `organizations:TagResource` grant reached `*`, which includes
policy resources. The attack is two steps and needs nothing but the delegated role:
tag central IT's SCP with `automat:managed-by=automat`, then modify or detach it
through the delegation policy, whose condition now passes. That is a complete
escape from the confinement the bundle advertises, using only the grants the bundle
ships, and central IT would have approved it after reading a README that told them
their policies were untouchable.

What made it hard to see is that **the two halves sit in different files**. The
delegation policy's conditions are correct in isolation. The role's tag grant is
unremarkable in isolation. Only the pair is exploitable, and a reviewer reads one
file at a time.

FIXED in `52e0ac1`: `organizations:TagResource` in the role now reaches no policy
resource at all and is confined by an `aws:TagKeys` condition that is `StringEquals`
over named keys rather than `StringLike` over `automat:*`; in the delegation policy,
`TagResource` reaches only resources that *already* carry
`automat:managed-by=automat`, so it cannot apply the key that would let it in. The
generated README now instructs central IT to read **both** `TagResource` statements
together, naming this as the check to make if they make only one — a mitigation
aimed at the fact that the defect was cross-file, not at the defect itself.

Held by `TestNoConditionReadsATagTheBundleLetsTheDelegateWrite`, which enumerates
every condition key the two policies *read* and asserts that no grant in either file
can *write* one — the cross-file invariant stated as a test rather than as a
paragraph, so a future grant that reintroduces this fails a test instead of relying
on a reader making the connection I initially missed. Supported by
`TestDelegateCannotTagItsWayIntoCentralITsPolicies`,
`TestVendorRoleCannotTagOutsideAutomatsNamespace`, `TestTheRoleNeverTouchesAPolicy`,
and `TestTheRoleCanOnlyTagAccountsItVended`.

## High

### H1 — `MoveAccount` may authorize against the source parent, which is not granted. ACCEPTED

The `MoveAccountsIntoTheDelegatedSubtreeOnly` statement lists `account/<org>/*` and
the target OU plus its descendants, and deliberately **omits the organization root**
— naming the root would permit the move the statement exists to prevent. If
Organizations authorizes `MoveAccount` against `SourceParentId` as well as
`DestinationParentId`, every vend fails, because a newly created account starts at
the root (DESIGN §5).

**ACCEPTED, recorded as Q9 in `docs/open-questions.md`.** It fails closed and
announces itself on the first live vend. Adding the root ARN now, to fix a failure
that may not exist, would widen the grant to permit parking any account at the root
— silently, in the direction that cannot be caught later. A denied first smoke vend
costs an afternoon; a role that can quietly move accounts out from under central
IT's SCPs is the exact failure this bundle is supposed to make impossible. Q9 names
the fallback if the answer is bad, and the Phase 5 runbook tests it first.

*A crabby auditor's signature:* accepted **because** the failure mode of being wrong
is a visible denial rather than a silent grant, and because the alternative
disposition trades a certain widening for an uncertain convenience.

### H2 — The delegate can detach the controls automat itself attached. FIXED (disclosure)

`scpAttachActions` includes `organizations:DetachPolicy` on automat-owned policies,
because a tool that can only ever attach cannot correct a bad policy. The
consequence the README did not state: the compliance controls automat attaches to a
vended account are **not permanent against the vending account**. Central IT reads
the blast-radius section and concludes those controls hold. They do not.

Not an escalation — central IT's own policies are tag-gated and their floor still
applies — but it falsifies an inference the document invites, which is the same class
of defect as AUDIT-0's H1 (emitting an unverifiable claim as a verified one).

FIXED in `541c3ec` as disclosure rather than a narrower grant, because the honest
remedy is re-checking state, not removing the tool's ability to correct itself. The
README now says so and names `automat verify` — **and says plainly that `verify` does
not ship in this version**. Offering a check the reader cannot run would be the worse
version of this finding: it invites approval on the strength of assurance that does
not exist yet. Held by `TestREADMEDisclosesWhatTheDelegateCanDoToAutomatsOwnControls`,
whose assertions are keyed to the actions the generated policy actually grants, so
removing a grant retires its disclosure requirement rather than leaving a paragraph
describing a permission that is gone.

### H3 — A role name could retype the YAML value it was rendered into. FIXED

An operator-supplied role name reaching an unquoted CFN scalar can change the
document's *type*, not just its content: a name that YAML reads as a boolean, a
number, a sexagesimal, or a `!!` tag produces a template whose `RoleName` is not a
string, and a name containing a newline plus indentation can introduce a sibling key.
The template is applied, so this is a grant-shaped injection, not a rendering bug.

FIXED in `256094a`: every operator value rendered into CFN is quoted and escaped
through a single YAML-string helper. The defense is layered and each layer has its
own test, because any one of them alone would be brittle:

- `TestValidateRejectsEveryInjectionShape` and `TestNoPatternAdmitsAStructuralCharacter`
  — no field pattern admits a newline, quote, brace, or other structural byte, so most
  payloads never reach a renderer at all.
- `TestNoTemplateSeesAnUnvalidatedValue` — every renderer validates before writing a
  byte, proven by handing each one a Request invalid in a single field (payload: a
  quote, a newline, and a forged `ManagedPolicyArns: [.../AdministratorAccess]`
  sibling key) and requiring both an error **and** no output. A renderer that
  interpolated first, or validated a copy, returns bytes here.
- `TestOperatorValuesSurviveTheYAMLParserAsStrings` and
  `TestNoOperatorValueLandsUnquotedInAStructuredField` — the rendered YAML is parsed
  and each operator value asserted to still be a *string*, which is the type-confusion
  half rather than the injection half.
- `TestRenderedFilesAreValidForTheirFormat` and `TestTemplatesCarryNoGoFormatVerb`
  round it out.

### H4 — Path resolution happened more than once, so the name could change underneath. FIXED

The bundle writer resolved the output path, then wrote to it by name — a TOCTOU
against the operator's own filesystem, and the concrete instance of the risk AUDIT-0's
A1 deferred. It matters here because one of the written files carries a live
ExternalId.

FIXED in `01d4b1a` and `77a9be5`: `openOutputDir` opens the *parent* and creates the
final component through that descriptor (`Root.Mkdir` + `Root.OpenRoot`), because the
obvious spelling — `MkdirAll(abs)` then `os.OpenRoot(abs)` — is wrong: `MkdirAll`
succeeds silently on an existing symlink and `OpenRoot` follows it, so every
"confined" write lands wherever the link points. All subsequent operations go through
the held root, and `internal/safeio` centralizes the discipline so the three former
copies cannot drift apart.

**This is the A1 revisit the human review made binding**; see the dedicated section
below, which records what `os.Root` does *not* do.

## Medium

### M1 — Uncapped `AttachPolicy` can exhaust the OU's five policy slots. FIXED (disclosure)

AWS permits five SCPs directly attached per target and AWS's own `FullAWSAccess`
occupies one (DESIGN.md:230). Nothing in the bundle caps how many the delegate
attaches to the delegated OU, so the delegate can fill it and central IT can then
attach nothing there.

It weakens no control — a full target still enforces everything attached to it, and
an SCP at a parent OU or the root still binds every account below, which is where an
institutional baseline belongs. But "I will attach my own policy to that OU later" is
a reasonable plan this breaks quietly. FIXED in `541c3ec` as disclosure, with the
remedy stated: attach it before handing over the delegation, or one level up.

### M2 — The README understated the read grant and ordered the apply steps dangerously. FIXED

Two defects in the same document, both of the "the doc is wrong about the code" class:

(a) The org-wide read grant was described as reading "the organization's structure",
which undersells it. Its 15 actions reach every account's **root-user email address**
and every SCP's **full text** — a map of what central IT's controls do and do not
cover. FIXED in `2057fc9`: stated plainly, with the reason it cannot be narrowed (an
Organizations account ARN does not encode the OU, so `account/<org>/*` is
unavoidably org-wide) so a reader can tell an unavoidable grant from a lazy one.

(b) The apply steps put "attach the delegation policy" before "check whether one
already exists". `PutResourcePolicy` replaces wholesale; following the steps in
order silently revokes any existing delegation. FIXED in the same commit: the check
is now step 1, marked destructive, with the read command and the note that the `Sid`s
are all `Automat`-prefixed so a hand-merged document cannot collide.

### M3 — Ambient credentials could satisfy the vendor-role trust path. FIXED

Confused-deputy review of the trust policy. Fixed alongside C1 in `52e0ac1`: the trust
policy names exactly one principal **and** requires `sts:ExternalId`, so knowing the
role ARN is insufficient. The README states this, and `TestCFNAndTFGrantTheSameThing`
asserts the `sts:ExternalId` condition is present in **both** the CFN and TF
renderings — an action granted with a condition in one file and without it in the
other is the same cross-file drift as C1, one step subtler, and it matters more here
because the operator deploys one file and never reads the other.
`TestNoRenderedFileLeaksTheExternalIDBeyondTheTrustPolicy` bounded the other side: the
value appeared exactly once per role template and zero times in the policy, `ou.md`,
and the README. **Superseded by the strictly stronger property** — the value now appears
*zero* times in every rendered file, asserted by
`TestNoRenderedFileContainsAnExternalIDValue`; that test was deleted rather than kept
alongside, because a test bounding where a secret may appear is meaningless once no
secret is there to bound.

### M4 — A resolved ExternalId was neither validated nor safely reported. FIXED

Two halves. A resolved ExternalId (from `env:` or `file:`) went into the trust policy
unchecked — so a value with a newline, a quote, or 4 KB of padding reached a policy
document. And a *rejected* value was echoed in the error message, printing a
credential to a terminal and probably a ticket.

FIXED in `4c2731c`: the resolved value is checked against AWS's documented ExternalId
charset and length before use, and rejected values are never echoed — `redactRef`
reports the *reference* (`env:VAR`, `file:/path`), not the secret. See N3 for the
residual nit.

### M5 — A placeholder ExternalId was accepted, and the docs overclaimed. FIXED

`--external-id changeme` (and friends) passed validation, producing a bundle whose
one anti-confused-deputy control is a value an attacker guesses first. Related: the
docs described the ExternalId as protecting more than it does.

FIXED in `894b6f9`: a placeholder corpus is refused, and the claim is now accurate —
it defends the role against assumption by a third party who learns the ARN; it is not
a password and does not defend against compromised credentials **in the trusted
account**.

**Superseded in part.** This finding assumed the bundle carries the value, and the
review's rejection of that design (see the human-review section) moved the placeholder
check to `internal/config`, where it now guards the value arriving *from* central IT
rather than one automat generated. The check itself is unchanged and still jam-checked;
`--external-id` is gone, so the CLI test that drove it became
`TestPreflightRefusesAWeakExternalID` against the resolver — the path a placeholder can
still travel.

### M6 — Unchecked writes meant a command could report success having written nothing. FIXED

Several writes that carry a command's actual answer ignored their error, and one
`Result.Dir` was reported without being confirmed. A `setup --request` that prints
"wrote 4 files" after a partial or failed write is worse than an error, because the
operator proceeds to send a bundle that is not there or not complete.

FIXED in `ef17c6f` and `5fab62e` (which also stopped the bundle vouching for a
property it did not have), and `73ab082` for `Result.Dir`.

## Low

### L1 — An unreachable-partition ARN could be constructed. FIXED

ARN parsing could accept a partition-inconsistent ARN from mixed inputs, and the
account-extraction logic existed at more than one call site — the shape where two
copies disagree about which field is the account. FIXED in `5fab62e` by routing it
through a single `arnAccount` helper (`internal/bundle/request.go:149`), with
`TestAGovCloudRoleARNIsAcceptedInTheRightAccount`,
`TestARoleARNInAnotherAccountIsStillRefused`, and
`TestARoleARNWithATraversalOrAnOverLongPathIsRefused` covering the partition,
cross-account, and traversal cases respectively.

### L2 — The config file was read with `os.ReadFile`, outside the safeio discipline. FIXED

Every other read went through the confined path; the config read did not, which is
how the "one of them ends up a version behind" failure starts. FIXED in `1dcda38`.

### L3 — An `--out` path containing a control byte was accepted. FIXED

A path with an embedded control character (including a newline or an ANSI escape)
was accepted and then echoed into terminal output — a path that can rewrite what the
operator sees about where their bundle went. FIXED in `73ab082`: refused, with the
offending byte named.

## Nits

### N1 — An OU name with a run of spaces reviews as a different name. FIXED

Markdown collapses whitespace in prose but **not inside a code fence**. So
`Research  Computing` renders identically to the single-spaced name in the README's
table and in ou.md's sentence, while ou.md's `--name` *argument* — inside a fence —
carries the doubled space. Central IT approves one name; a different OU is created.
Filed as a nit because it needs an operator to supply the name, but it is a genuine
review-integrity defect: the reviewed artifact and the executed one differ. FIXED in
`b973b2c`.

### N2 — An identifier-shaped OU name collides with ou.md's whole purpose. FIXED

`ou.md` exists to teach central IT to tell an OU name from an OU id, an org id, a
root id, and the substitution placeholder. An OU *name* in one of those forms puts
both meanings into the same instruction. FIXED in `b973b2c`, case-insensitively, as a
check separate from `reOUName` — narrowing `reOUName` would have rejected
`Research-1`, which is legitimate. The test's **accepted** half (nine ordinary names
including `CUI Enclave`, `dept.research`, `Research 800-171`) is the part that makes
this worth having rather than merely strict.

### N3 — `redactRef` leaks a byte of the secret when the scheme is malformed. FIXED-ish. ACCEPTED

For a reference with no recognized scheme, `redactRef` renders up to the first colon.
A reference typed as `SECRETVALUE` with no colon therefore reports nothing, but one
typed `secret:value` reports `secret:` — one token of an already-malformed reference.
**ACCEPTED:** the leaked text is the part the operator wrote as a scheme, the message
exists to tell them the scheme was wrong, and redacting it entirely produces "your
reference is malformed" with no way to see which one. The residual exposure is
bounded by a colon the operator chose.

## The A1 revisit — binding from the AUDIT-0 human review

AUDIT-0 accepted three `G304` sites conditionally: **"this becomes a real finding the
moment a path is derived from a profile, a bundle, or an API response"**, with
`os.Root` named as the remedy. Phase 1 is that moment, so this is the required
revisit rather than a re-acceptance.

**What changed.** Every path that Phase 1 writes or reads under operator control now
goes through `internal/safeio` and an `os.Root` held as an open directory descriptor
(H4). That covers the bundle output directory, the SSO token cache, the config file,
and ExternalId `file:` references.

**What `os.Root` does not do.** Re-verified for this audit with a throwaway probe
against **both go1.24.0 (the module floor) and go1.26.5 (the current toolchain)** on
darwin/arm64, identical results, rather than repeated from the comment that asserts
it — the claim is load-bearing for H4, and a code comment is not evidence:

- It refuses a symlink whose target **escapes** the root
  (`openat <name>: path escapes from parent`).
- It **follows** a symlink whose target is **inside** the root — returns `nil`.
- It silently **ignores** `syscall.O_NOFOLLOW` in `OpenFile` flags: `Root.OpenFile`
  returns `nil` where plain `os.OpenFile` on the same path returns
  `too many levels of symbolic links`.

So `os.Root` bounds *where* a redirected write lands but does not answer "am I
opening the file I just looked at". `safeio` answers that separately, comparing the
opened descriptor against the preceding `Lstat` with `os.SameFile` and refusing the
operation if the identity differs. Confinement and identity are two properties and
`os.Root` provides one.

Two API constraints worth recording, both consequences of the **Go 1.24 floor**:
`os.Root.WriteFile` and `os.Root.Rename` are Go 1.25, so writes go through
`Root.OpenFile` + `O_TRUNC` rather than write-to-temp-and-rename, and `Root.Readlink`
is likewise unavailable. If the floor moves to 1.25, revisit — rename-into-place is
the stronger pattern and is currently unavailable, not rejected.

**The three original G304 sites remain, and remain ACCEPTED**, now for a narrower
reason than in AUDIT-0. `gen/catalog/sources.go:285` and `gen/catalog/main.go:39` are
a maintainer-run tool reading its own source tree — not shipped in the binary, not
reachable by an operator, let alone a remote. `internal/artifact/load.go:95` is
`Load` reading the path its caller names; **`cmd/automat` has no caller of it in
Phase 1** (verified: the only config read is `config.Load` via `globals.go:62`, which
goes through safeio). The condition AUDIT-0 attached has therefore not yet been met
for that site, and the honest disposition is to carry the condition forward rather
than declare it resolved:

> **Binding on AUDIT-2/3:** the first time `artifact.Load` is called with a path from
> a profile, a catalog reference, or anything but a CLI flag, it must move to a
> `safeio`-confined read. Phase 2's profiles are the likely trigger.

`A2` (0644/0755 on the published catalog) stands as written in AUDIT-0; nothing in
Phase 1 changes the argument, and the artifact is still a public, hash-verified
document rather than a secret.

## Other ritual scope items

### TOCTOU between preflight and mutating actions — NO FINDING YET, and the code says so

AUDIT-0's A7 required AUDIT-1 to confirm the code does not treat a preflight pass as
an authorization. It does not, and this is structural rather than a caveat in prose:
`preflight` distinguishes **Simulated** from **Observed** results in its own type
(`preflight.go:74`), because DESIGN §3 fact 9 means `iam:SimulatePrincipalPolicy`
does not evaluate SCPs — so a simulated allow may be shadowed by exactly the policies
that matter, while a simulated deny is reliable. The asymmetry is in the data model,
and the printed report carries the caveat whenever any check is simulated
(`preflight.go:719`, `cmd/automat/preflight.go:41`).

No mutating AWS call exists yet, so the actual TOCTOU window — check, then act —
cannot be audited. **Binding on AUDIT-2**, when `vend` introduces it: the question is
whether a preflight result is ever *reused* as authorization for a later action
rather than re-established at the point of the call.

### The evidence chain — NOTHING TO AUDIT YET

Unchanged from AUDIT-0's A8. No manifest is read or written and no signer exists
(Phase 3).

### The SCP packer — NOTHING TO AUDIT YET

`internal/compilesets` does not exist (Phase 4), so "can any merge WIDEN permissions"
remains unanswerable in code. AUDIT-0's H3/H4/H5 answered three instances of the
question at the schema layer, which is where the cheap answers live; the expensive
one is still ahead.

### Error and log paths — reviewed, one finding

Credential, ARN, and email leakage into errors and output was reviewed as a class.
M4 (echoing a rejected ExternalId) was the finding; N3 is its residue. ARNs and
account ids *are* printed, deliberately — CLAUDE.md rule 7 requires a permission
failure to name which action, which resource, and what grant would fix it, and an
ARN is the resource. An account id is not a secret; the ExternalId is the only value
in Phase 1 that is.

## Dependency review

`govulncheck ./...`: **no vulnerabilities found.** `gosec`: 8 issues, all triaged —
3× G304 (the A1 section above), G301/G302 (AUDIT-0 A2, unchanged), and three in
`internal/login`:

- **G505/G401 — `crypto/sha1`** (`login.go:44,514`). Interop, not integrity: the SSO
  token cache filename is the SHA-1 of the start URL because that is the filename the
  AWS CLI computes, and the point is to share a cache with it. Nothing verifies
  anything with this hash; a collision produces a cache miss. **ACCEPTED.** A
  comment at each site says so, so a future reader does not "fix" it and silently
  stop sharing the cache.
- **G117 — marshaling a struct with an `AccessToken` field** (`login.go:385`). Correct
  and unavoidable: this *is* the token cache, and writing the token is its purpose.
  The relevant controls are that it is written 0600 through a confined root with the
  identity check (H4), and that automat stores no other secret (DESIGN §13).
  **ACCEPTED.**

**Two directly-imported modules were marked `// indirect`** in `go.mod` —
`BurntSushi/toml` and `aws-sdk-go-v2/config`. Not a vulnerability, but a real defect
*of the dependency review itself*: a module the code imports directly, filed under
"indirect", is one a reviewer reads past. Fixed by `go mod tidy` in the commit
carrying this file.

No dependency was added beyond those CLAUDE.md names, with one exception requiring
ratification (below). Outdated indirect modules exist (`golang.org/x/{mod,sys,text,tools}`
at old versions, pulled by `jsonschema/v6`), none reported vulnerable and none but
`x/text` reaching the built binary; `x/text`'s newer versions are available and
uneventful. Left alone deliberately: bumping transitive dependencies with no vuln and
no bug is churn that costs review attention, which is the scarce resource here.

## For the human to review — ratification requests

Three items need an explicit yes, per CLAUDE.md's "ask the human before" rule. All
three were made during the phase; none is retroactively self-approved.

1. **Dependency: `github.com/aws/aws-sdk-go-v2/config`.** Not in CLAUDE.md's named
   set. Required by DESIGN §13's "lean on the AWS credential chain" — that chain *is*
   `config.LoadDefaultConfig`, and hand-rolling credential resolution to avoid the
   import would be strictly worse security. Same SDK, same maintainer, no new
   third-party surface.

2. **CLI surface added.** Listed in full rather than summarized, since "plus the
   `setup --request` inputs" would be asking for ratification of something not shown:

   - Persistent: `--config`, `--context`.
   - `login`: `--start-url`, `--sso-region`.
   - `preflight`: none.
   - `setup`: `--request`, `--dry-run`, `--force`, `--out`, `--org`, `--ou`,
     `--ou-name`, `--management-account`, `--member-account`, `--member-role-arn`,
     `--vendor-role-name`, `--external-id`, `--contact`.

   The surface is new rather than altered — no flag from an earlier phase changed
   meaning — but CLAUDE.md requires asking either way. Two are worth a second look
   because they are the ones with security semantics: `--external-id` (documented for
   re-sending a bundle for an already-deployed role; omitting it is the normal path
   and generates one) and `--force` (overwrites files edited by hand, which is how an
   operator's local correction gets silently discarded).

   **Ratified on the condition that it matches DESIGN §13, with deviations flagged as
   their own line items. Reconciliation done: `docs/cli-surface.md`.** Read from the
   built binary and from `internal/config`'s struct tags rather than from this list —
   checking §13 against a summary of the code would find nothing. Result: every §13
   command is either shipped or unbuilt-and-phased, all four §13 config keys exist,
   and §13's "never store secrets" holds literally. Two things came out of it:

   - **`--external-id` has since been removed** (review item 6). Not a §13 deviation
     in either direction: §13's command list never mentioned it, and §13's "never
     store secrets" is what removing it serves. Recorded because the review ratified
     a list that has since shrunk, and that should be visible without diffing audits.
   - **D1, a real deviation, found by doing this rather than asserting it** — see
     below.

3. **Validation changes that strictly tighten.** Per the amended rule 6 these did not
   need pre-approval, and are listed here for ratification: resolved-ExternalId
   charset and length (M4), the weak/placeholder `--external-id` corpus (M5), ARN
   traversal and length limits (L1), the `--out` control-byte refusal (L3), and the
   two OU-name checks (N1, N2). **No schema file changed in Phase 1** — these are Go
   validators, so `schema/CHANGELOG.md` is untouched.

## For the human to review — ACCEPTED items

Two new findings, plus two AUDIT-0 acceptances re-examined. Each with a reason a
crabby auditor would sign:

**And one trade that was not a finding but was the human's call, not mine — REJECTED
BY REVIEW, now FIXED:**

**The bundle contained a live `sts:ExternalId`.** Both role templates embedded it, so
the bundle was a sensitive file that had to be sent over a channel the operator trusts.
I argued the alternative was worse — a placeholder each side invents means the two sides
must configure the *same* value, so it guarantees either an out-of-band exchange done
over email anyway, or a mismatch surfacing as a confusing `AccessDenied` during the
first vend — and recorded it here because the mitigation was a paragraph, and a
paragraph is a weaker control than a mechanism.

**The review did not accept it, and was right.** The instruction: *"NOT accepted as a
paragraph. Convert to a mechanism by inverting generation."*

My framing contained the error. I posed the choice as "automat generates the value" vs.
"a placeholder each side invents", and both options leave the *requester* choosing the
management account's confused-deputy defense. There was a third option I did not
consider: the bundle carries no value at all, and the party bearing the risk generates
its own. Central IT is already applying a template; supplying one parameter while doing
so is not an out-of-band exchange bolted on, it is a line in the command they were
running regardless. The cost I priced as prohibitive was mostly imaginary.

The mechanism, FIXED in this phase:

- Both templates declare the value as a deploy-time input — CloudFormation
  `AutomatExternalId` with `NoEcho: true`, Terraform `var.automat_external_id` with
  `sensitive = true`, both bounded by `MinLength`/`MaxLength`/`AllowedPattern` derived
  from `internal/config`'s exported constants so the template cannot accept a value the
  resolver will later refuse.
- `Request.ExternalID`, `bundle.NewExternalID`, and `setup --external-id` are deleted.
  There is no field, no generator, and no flag — nothing for a future edit to route
  back into a rendered file.
- The placeholder and charset validators moved to `internal/config`, which is now the
  only side that exists: "the requester typed `changeme`" became "somebody typed
  `changeme`" without becoming any less likely.
- The README's disclosure paragraph became three numbered steps with an
  `openssl rand -hex 24`, plus the instruction with teeth: *do not accept an ExternalId
  from the requester.*
- `TestNoRenderedFileContainsAnExternalIDValue` is the mechanism replacing the
  paragraph. It is keyed to the right-hand side of the `sts:ExternalId` assignment
  rather than to a known value — there is no known value to search for, and a test
  looking for a specific string would pass on any *other* secret in the same slot.
  `TestBothTemplatesDeclareTheExternalIDAsADeployTimeInput` stops the trivial way to
  satisfy it (delete the condition entirely).

**Two things this change surfaced that the original finding did not mention:**

1. **The Terraform `sensitive = true` keyword does not do what it looks like.** It keeps
   the value out of plan output; it does **not** keep it out of state, and nothing can,
   because the trust policy is part of the resource. The generated variable description
   says so outright rather than letting the keyword imply more than it delivers. A
   reader who trusts `sensitive` to mean "not persisted" is worse off than one who was
   told nothing.
2. **`internal/bundle/write.go`'s entire threat model was ExternalId confidentiality,
   and removing the secret removed its stated reason for every protection it has.** The
   protections were kept, on a better argument that was true all along: these five files
   are a *grant* that central IT reads and applies, so what matters is the integrity of
   the file at review time and at apply time. Anyone who can redirect one of these
   writes does not learn a secret — they choose the IAM policy someone else is about to
   deploy, which is strictly worse than the leak the file was organized around. The
   confidentiality argument for 0600/0700/`.gitignore` did genuinely weaken; those are
   now labelled as conservative defaults for a tool's own output instead of borrowing
   urgency from a retired threat.

   The generated `.gitignore` was the sharpest instance, because it is *output*: it told
   every operator "this directory contains an sts:ExternalId, which is a shared secret",
   which had become false. A false warning in a generated file is not a stale comment; it
   trains the reader to discount the warnings that are true.

### D1 — The CLI promised the wrong release for `setup` without `--request`. FIXED

Found by the DESIGN §13 reconciliation the review asked for (item 2), which is the
argument for having asked: the surface *matched* §13, and the defect was in a string

`automat setup` in a management account is unimplemented, and the refusal told the

Low severity, and worth a finding anyway. It is a promise to an operator who is
blocked, in the one place they will look, naming a release that will not contain what
they are waiting for. It appeared in the `Long` help, the refusal message, the command's

`Contains(err, "Phase 2")`, which passed while being wrong, and would have passed for any
phase number after a correction. CLAUDE.md's rule (when design and code disagree, flag
it rather than reinterpret) makes ROADMAP the sequencing authority, so the code was
wrong, not the ROADMAP.

## Where this audit is weaker than it looks

Recorded because an audit that only reports what it found is itself a claim outrunning
its evidence.

- **Two overlapping defenses were jam-checked and found to overlap.** The `O_NONBLOCK`
  FIFO guard in `safeio` and the regular-file check protect against related cases, so
  breaking one does not always fail a test; likewise parts of the login coverage table.
  The tests pass, but they pin the pair rather than each member, and I could not
  construct a jam that isolated them.
- **Two `cmd/automat` tests initially overstated what they pinned.** One subtest
  `Skipf`'d its way past the case it was named for — an empty `orgFake.ResourcePolicy`
  is an *observed failure*, not an unknown, so the "undetermined alone" fixture never
  reached the branch it claimed to test. Fixed in `42b6829` by building the fixture
  deliberately (management caller, all five simulated actions, non-empty resource
  policy) and replacing `Skipf` with `Fatalf`: a fixture that goes stale now fails
  loudly instead of quietly testing nothing. A test that skips is not a test, and I
  wrote one.
- **Everything about live AWS behavior is untested by construction.** Q5–Q9 in
  `docs/open-questions.md` are the honest list. The delegation model's central claims
  — that the tag conditions bind as intended, that `MoveAccount` authorizes where
  expected — rest on reading AWS documentation, not on observing AWS. Fakes cannot
  falsify them, and a green test suite here is not evidence about them.

### One agent finding recorded as WRONG

An adversarial pass reported `GeneratedAt` as an unvalidated field reaching the

`time.Now().UTC()` at `cmd/automat/setup.go:103`, not from operator input. Recorded
